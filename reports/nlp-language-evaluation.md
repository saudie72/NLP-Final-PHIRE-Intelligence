**Domain errors.** Some VADER lexicon words are neutral PeopleSoft vocabulary: "save" (+), "security" (+), "effective" (+), "value" (+), "created" (+), "support" (+), "approved" (+), "pay" (−), "po" (−). "Pay" in a payroll ticket is not a complaint, and "security" in an access request is not praise, yet both move the score.

**Finding.** VADER was built for social-media text. On these tickets it labels 122 positive, 94 negative and 34 neutral: politeness and request words (see the positive drivers) push routine requests positive, while problem words push others negative. PeopleSoft urgency signals such as "queued", "abend" or "pay confirmation" are not in its lexicon. VADER separates low-priority tickets (higher mean) from the rest but is a weak priority signal on its own; the urgency vocabulary below is stronger, so the app shows both and keeps the rule-based priority suggestion.

## spaCy: EntityRuler for PeopleSoft objects

With the EntityRuler added before the statistical NER, 77% of tickets mention at least one PeopleSoft module, page, process or tool. What `en_core_web_sm` alone calls those same spans:

**Finding.** The general-purpose model has never seen PeopleSoft: it labels pages and processes as ORG, PERSON, PRODUCT or misses them entirely. The EntityRuler fixes this with rules from the research docs. Remaining errors are terms outside the pattern list and capitalised business words the model still tags (see below).

## Limitations

- Synthetic, template-generated text: real tickets will be noisier and VADER and the EntityRuler will miss more.
- The EntityRuler only knows the terms listed in `pipeline/patterns.py`; new pages and processes need new patterns.
- spaCy runs only at build time (it cannot run in the browser), so spaCy results cover the demo dataset; NLTK also runs live in the browser through Pyodide for any text.
